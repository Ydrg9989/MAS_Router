# Pre-registration: RQ2–RQ5, and the go/no-go on delegation

Written **before** any of the four experiments is run, in the style of D-026 and the pool-sweep
pre-registration. Every prediction has a refutation branch and a stated consequence, and the
go/no-go rule is fixed here so that the direction decision is mechanical rather than a judgement
call made after seeing the numbers.

Status: **not yet run.** Predictions and thresholds locked 2026-08-13.

---

## 1. Why these four, and why now

`Docs/literature/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md` §24 defines five research questions for the delegation
direction. Only RQ1 and RQ2 have ever been tested:

- **RQ1** — retired (D-030). The criterion passed identically on shuffled noise, and the
  nearest-neighbour routing test read the labels.
- **RQ2** — closed for *aggregation* families (D-033, D-040): a learned `q(x, S, p)` gains
  −0.01 pp over 70 `crosscap240` pools and −0.46 pp over 210 `hard366` pools.

But **every routing result in this project chose among 15 coalitions × 2 aggregation rules.** The
five priced protocols — `independent_judge`, `expert_verifier`, `expert_veto`, `debate_vote`,
`chair_information_seeking` — have never been in a router's choice set. RQ3, RQ4 and RQ5 were
never built at all. Four of five research questions are therefore unrefuted because they were
never run, and the direction cannot honestly be closed or continued until they are.

All four are computable from banked data. **Cost: $0.**

## 2. The experiments

### RQ2′ — routing when the choice set includes *interaction*, not only aggregation

The delegation decision the field cares about is MasRouter's "collaboration mode": should these
agents vote, or should they debate? Ours has never been asked it.

**Design.** On `hard366`, for each of the three named pools, build the organization family
`15 coalitions × {independent_majority, single_expert}` **plus** the five priced protocols on the
grand coalition = **35 organizations**. Restrict to the tasks all 35 ran, which is the pool's
discriminating subset (129–210 tasks). Fit `q_θ` and the frozen baseline ladder exactly as
`routing.py` does today, over stratified resplits.

**The controlled comparison.** Run the same pipeline on the same task subset with the 30-organization
family. The difference between the two isolates the protocol axis rather than the task subset, which
a naive comparison against the published 366-task numbers would confound.

### RQ3 — is dense counterfactual supervision worth collecting?

This is a question about *data*, not about routers, and the two-stage harness is the only apparatus
here that can pose it: a real deployment logs **one** organization per task, whereas this project has
**all thirty**.

**Design.** Fix a supervision budget of `N` observed `(task, organization)` cells and fill it two ways:

- **dense** — pick `N/30` tasks and observe all 30 organizations on each;
- **observational** — pick `N` tasks and observe one uniformly-random organization on each, which is
  the shape of an ordinary execution log.

Fit `q_θ` on each arm using **only observed cells** — including the competence features, which must
be computed from observed singletons alone or the dense arm's advantage leaks into the sparse one.
Score both by routing accuracy on held-out tasks. Sweep `N` over the pre-registered grid
`{0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50, 1.00}` of the full grid size.

### RQ4 — does any of it generalize?

Every routing number in this project comes from an **IID** split. Three holdouts, all harder:

- **domain holdout** — the `lodo_train::` / `lodo_test::` splits already built into every manifest
  and never read by any script. Train on all domains but one, route on the held-out domain.
- **agent holdout** — hold out one agent; fit on organizations that do not contain it; route on test
  tasks over organizations that do.
- **organization holdout** — fit on a random 60% of organizations; route on test tasks over the
  40% never seen during fitting.

Each is scored against the same frozen fixed-best baseline, fitted under the same restriction.

### RQ5 — can it tell when *not* to collaborate?

**Design.** Reduce the decision to a binary: for each test task, take the best singleton organization
and the best multi-member organization, both frozen on calibration, and choose one. Compare the
learned rule against always-solo, always-collaborate, and the per-task oracle over the two.

## 3. Controls that ship with every arm

Non-negotiable, because D-029 and D-030 both died of their absence:

1. **A shuffled-embedding twin** for every learned rule. Same model, same choice set, task–embedding
   association destroyed. A gain the twin also achieves is not a gain.
2. **A frozen baseline**, fitted on the same training information as the rule it is compared against
   and never on test outcomes.
3. **Resplits**, not one partition. D-033 showed a single split produces two false findings at these
   sample sizes.
4. **Scale.** RQ3, RQ4 and RQ5 run on all 70 `crosscap240` pools, not three. RQ2′ runs on the three
   `hard366` pools, because that is all the priced data that exists.

## 4. Pre-registered predictions

| # | Quantity | Prediction | Refuted if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q2a** | `q_θ` mean gain over frozen fixed-best on the 35-organization family | ≤ 0 pp, as on the 30-organization family | ≥ +1.0 pp and ahead in ≥ 60% of resplits |
| **Q2b** | Gain on 35 organizations minus gain on 30, same tasks | ≈ 0; adding the protocol axis does not create routable structure | ≥ +1.0 pp |
| **Q3a** | Dense minus observational routing accuracy at matched budget | dense ≥ observational, because cross-organization contrasts are informative | dense < observational at every budget |
| **Q3b** | Dense arm's own gain over frozen fixed-best | ≤ 0 at every budget — better supervision of a target that is not there buys nothing | ≥ +1.0 pp at any budget |
| **Q4** | `q_θ` mean gain under domain, agent and organization holdout | ≤ 0 in all three, and no better than under the IID split | ≥ +1.0 pp in ≥ 2 of 3 regimes |
| **Q5** | Learned solo-vs-collaborate rule minus the better fixed policy | ≤ 0 | ≥ +1.0 pp and ahead in ≥ 60% of resplits |

**+1.0 pp is the effect size that matters** and it is set here rather than after the fact. It is
roughly half the selection-variance cost measured in D-040 (2.42 pp) and roughly a tenth of the
phantom headroom (10.0 pp). An effect below it cannot be distinguished from the noise that D-033
showed swamps single-split routing numbers, and would not justify a paper.

## 5. The go/no-go rule, fixed in advance

**GO** on continuing the delegation direction if **at least one** of Q2a, Q2b, Q3b, Q4, Q5 is
refuted in the direction of routing working, **and** the corresponding shuffled-embedding twin does
**not** clear the same bar, **and** the effect holds on a majority of the pools tested.

All three conditions are required. Any one alone has already produced a false positive in this
project: D-029 (no null), D-030 (no leakage check), D-033 (single split).

**NO-GO** on RQ2-style delegation — "a learned model picks the organization per task" — if none of
those five clears the bar. In that case the four experiments become the *scope* section of whichever
paper is written, and the direction's remaining value is the selection-variance diagnosis rather than
the router.

**Q3a is deliberately outside the go/no-go.** It can come out either way without bearing on whether
routing works, because it is a claim about data collection. If dense supervision beats observational
logs it is a reportable methodological finding regardless of the routing verdict, and the pool sweep
already shows the routing verdict is negative.

---

## 6. Why no outcome wastes the time

- **Everything confirmed** → four of five research questions move from "never tested" to "tested and
  negative", the direction closes honestly and completely, and the negative becomes citable rather
  than an admission. The selection-variance diagnosis (D-040) becomes the surviving contribution.
- **Q2a or Q2b refuted** → routing pays once the choice set contains interaction protocols, which is
  a *positive* result on the axis MasRouter occupies, and the direction reopens with a clear target.
- **Q4 refuted** → the router generalizes where it does not interpolate, which would be a genuinely
  surprising and publishable structure finding.
- **Q5 refuted** → "when not to collaborate" is learnable even though "which collaboration" is not,
  which is a narrower but real delegation claim and matches the one robust fact in the project
  (whole-pool voting is hard to beat).
- **Q3b refuted** → the negative was a supervision problem all along, and D-033's sample-size defence
  was tested on the wrong axis.

The only uninformative outcome would be running these without controls, which §3 forbids.

## 7. Implementation notes

- Reuse `mas_harness/metrics/routing.py` primitives unchanged — `_organization_features`,
  `_task_features`, `_pair_matrix`, `_model_scores`, `_best_row` — so the leak discipline is the same
  instrument, not a lookalike. New code supplies grids and splits, never a second model.
- Reuse `mas_harness/metrics/pool_sweep.py` to build the 70-pool substrate; the section 9 gate of the
  pool-sweep pre-registration already certifies that reconstruction.
- One BLAS thread per worker process. D-040 measured a several-fold slowdown without it.
- Cost: **$0**. No model call is made by any arm.
